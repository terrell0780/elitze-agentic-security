# ELITZE 2026 — Public Agency Capability-Gap Matrix

## Important framing

Public sources cannot establish what an agency privately wishes it had. This matrix therefore records **publicly stated operational gaps and requirements** from five major national cyber-security agencies and maps them to the ELITZE opportunity.

## 1. CISA (United States)

The 2026 joint agentic-AI guidance authored with partner agencies calls for strict privilege controls, continuous monitoring, strong identity management, human oversight and alignment with existing security frameworks. It also calls for continuous visibility and assurance over agentic AI deployments.

**ELITZE opportunity:** operationalize these requirements as a continuously evaluated control/evidence graph rather than a static checklist.

## 2. NSA (United States)

NSA co-authored the 2026 joint guidance on careful adoption of agentic AI with CISA, ASD/ACSC, the Canadian Cyber Centre and the UK/NZ NCSC organizations. The guidance emphasizes threat modelling, incident response, governance and centralized policy decision points for autonomous actions.

**ELITZE opportunity:** connect policy decisions to identity, reachability, action authorization, response and post-action verification.

## 3. NCSC-UK

NCSC's 2026 guidance emphasizes understanding dependencies, monitoring agent behaviour across tools and connected systems, threat modelling, and planning for incidents involving agentic AI failures, misuse or loss of control.

**ELITZE opportunity:** make the dependency/behaviour/incident chain a live security graph with evidence state and explicit uncertainty.

## 4. ASD / ACSC (Australia)

ASD's September 2026 agentic-AI harness guidance states that gaps in agentic-AI cybersecurity tooling and immature standards amplify risk. It identifies the harness and surrounding ecosystem as a primary long-term security and governance focus. ASD's updated system-access controls also require unique AI-agent identities and a maintained AI-agent register containing ownership, purpose, identities, credentials, tools, permissions and data access.

**ELITZE opportunity:** provide the operational register, identity-to-action relationship, policy enforcement, reachability and verification layer around the harness ecosystem.

## 5. Canadian Centre for Cyber Security

The Cyber Centre's 2026 frontier-AI guidance recommends zero-trust treatment of non-human identities, including AI agents, with dynamic credentials and privilege boundaries. Its joint agentic-AI guidance emphasizes layered defence, access controls and secure operation. Its Five Eyes statement stresses that rapid AI change requires adaptation and integrated cyber strategy.

**ELITZE opportunity:** turn non-human identity, access, exposure, runtime action and evidence into one continuously evaluated security decision surface.

## Common denominator

Across these agencies, the public requirements converge on:

- **Visibility:** know what agents exist and what they can reach.
- **Identity:** distinguish each agent from its initiating human and other agents.
- **Least privilege:** constrain access to the minimum required scope.
- **Central policy:** evaluate consequential actions against explicit authorization.
- **Monitoring:** observe behaviour across tools, workflows and connected systems.
- **Human oversight:** require intervention for higher-impact actions.
- **Incident readiness:** detect, contain, recover and learn from agent compromise.
- **Assurance:** continuously validate that controls still work.
- **Evidence:** preserve enough context to explain and defend decisions.

## ELITZE differentiation

Do not market ELITZE as replacing CISA, NSA, NCSC, ASD/ACSC or the Canadian Cyber Centre. Market it as an **operational control and evidence layer that helps organizations implement the kinds of controls these agencies are publicly calling for.**

The strongest ELITZE loop is:

**Incident Alert → ELITZE Triage → Policy Decision → SOC Action → SOC Support → Retest → Verified Evidence**

The product's differentiator is the closed loop: the system should not stop when an action is issued; it should establish whether the authorized action changed the security state and preserve the evidence.
