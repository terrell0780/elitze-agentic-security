# 2026 AI Terms Change Log and Regulatory Basis

**Reviewed:** September 3, 2026

This file records external developments used to shape the ELITZE 2026 AI terms. It is a research record, not a legal opinion and not a statement that ELITZE or any customer is compliant with a law.

## External developments reviewed

### EU AI Act — transparency and GPAI enforcement
The European Commission states that AI Act Article 50 transparency obligations apply from August 2, 2026, including obligations concerning disclosure of AI interaction and labeling of certain AI-generated or altered content. The Commission also states that enforcement of general-purpose AI provider obligations begins August 2, 2026.

Source: European Commission, “Commission starts enforcing AI Act rules and new transparency requirements on 2 August,” July 31, 2026.

### EU AI Act — GPAI provider documentation
The Commission states that GPAI providers have obligations concerning technical documentation, downstream documentation, copyright policies, training-content summaries, and, for systemic-risk models, additional risk-management, incident-reporting, and cybersecurity obligations.

Source: European Commission, “Guidelines on obligations for General-Purpose AI providers.”

### OpenAI enterprise terms
OpenAI’s current Services Agreement is effective January 1, 2026 and separates business/developer service terms from consumer terms. OpenAI also maintains a DPA effective January 1, 2026.

Source: OpenAI Services Agreement and OpenAI Data Processing Addendum.

### OpenAI enterprise data handling
OpenAI states that business and API data is not used for model training by default, subject to its published policies and applicable settings/agreements.

Source: OpenAI Business Data Privacy, Security, and Compliance; API data-usage policy.

### Provider-specific AI acceptable-use policies
Google maintains service-specific generative-AI terms and a prohibited-use policy addressing illegal activity, privacy, intellectual-property rights, and other restricted uses. This reinforces the need for ELITZE contracts to distinguish ELITZE controls from third-party provider terms.

Source: Google Generative AI Prohibited Use Policy and service-specific terms.

## ELITZE contract changes derived from this review

1. Add explicit AI-system and agent disclosures.
2. Define customer responsibility for high-impact decisions and human oversight.
3. Define authorized scope and accountability for agentic actions.
4. Address AI output uncertainty, non-uniqueness, and IP responsibility.
5. Address third-party model/provider terms and data-routing responsibility.
6. Add AI-generated-content disclosure and labeling responsibility.
7. Define REO provenance, evidence, uniqueness, risk, and publishing decisions as control outcomes rather than legal conclusions.
8. Add ongoing regulatory/provider-term change management.
9. Keep privacy, DPA, security, retention, and incident commitments separable from general AI terms.
